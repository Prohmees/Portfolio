What I studied: Applied Geology

Applied Geology uses the science of rock, soil and fluids to solve practical problems: where to look for oil and gas, how a formation behaves under load, and whether ground can carry a structure.

My degree covered both sides of that question, the petroleum side and the engineering side. Everything below is a layer of that course, and each one shows up in the work I've done since.

Core courses

Select a course to see what it covers and where I've used it.

Petroleum Geology

How oil and gas form, move through rock and get trapped, and how to judge whether a prospect is worth drilling.

In my work: reservoir characterisation projects at Platform Petroleum Limited.

Structural Geology

How rock layers fold, tilt and fracture. Faults and folds decide where hydrocarbons collect and how stable the ground is.

In my work: spotting structural features on subsurface contour maps and geological cross-sections.

Sedimentology

How sediment is carried, deposited and turned into rock, and how to read past environments from what was left behind.

In my work: identifying stratigraphic features while building cross-sections.

Geophysics

Imaging what's underground without digging, using seismic waves and other measurements.

In my work: seismic interpretation in Petrel.

Stratigraphy

Ordering rock layers in time and matching them from one place to another.

In my work: well log correlation across wells.

Soil Mechanics

How soil carries load, compacts, drains and fails. It is the basis for safe foundations.

In my work: applying soil and rock mechanics to formation stability assessments, and building skills in Atterberg limits, Proctor compaction, CBR and shear strength testing.

Engineering Geology

Applying geology to construction and infrastructure: reading ground conditions before anything is built on them.

In my work: site investigation, borehole log interpretation and ground characterisation.
